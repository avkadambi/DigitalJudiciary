How to read a case

Each entry is a headnote generated from the executed trace, not from an authored expectation. Why this case exists states the mechanism it was constructed to exercise; what happens states what the reference engine did with it; the tags at the foot name the axioms of the architectural contract (A1–A9) and the conformance checks (VC-01–VC-26) it exercises.

A case bordered in crimson engages an absolute provision — short-circuited at the first instance, never risk-acceptable at any tier, exit only by amendment. The word is doing unusual work here, and the distinction is about authority: actuating a customer's cooling setpoints is not a risk this vendor's engineers can accept, because the risk is not theirs. It belongs to the customer whose floor is at stake.

A case marked shipped engages one of the twenty-nine provisions of the released DCVigil pack, reproduced here with its text and absolute flag unchanged so that the 144 shipped cases remain valid. Five findings against those provisions — including that the seven-year audit retention rests on no external instrument — are filed as draft amendments rather than applied, because an adjudicator may not edit the rulebook and neither may a corpus author.

The corpus, its constitution, the reference engine, the conformance suite and the mutation matrix are open source: Apache-2.0 for code, CC BY 4.0 for data.
