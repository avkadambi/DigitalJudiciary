# 04 — Computational Legal Reasoning Corpora & Regulatory/Veterinary-Legal Reference Material

**Lane:** factor-based CBR corpora + legal NLP benchmarks + regulatory source text for a "veterinary constitution"
**Target system:** Digital Judiciary — executable institution with an admission rule, a distinguishing gate (premise-subset test), a lexicographic ordering over precedents, and binding precedent records.
**Date of verification:** 2026-09-05. Every license below was checked by visiting the source. Where a license could **not** be verified, it is marked `UNVERIFIED — do not vendor`. Nothing here is guessed.

The single most important finding: **there is no downloadable, licensed release of the CATO / HYPO US Trade Secret factor case base.** The factor vocabulary and the case outcomes are documented across ~30 years of copyrighted papers, but no canonical machine-readable corpus with a license exists. That gap has to be filled by *construction*, not by *acquisition* — and the veterinary domain is a legitimate place to do it.

### 1.2 Bottom line for the factor lane

There is **nothing safe to vendor**. The correct move is to build a *native veterinary factor vocabulary* (VCPR-established, in-person-exam-performed, extralabel-in-food-animal, withdrawal-period-assigned, controlled-substance-schedule, PDMP-queried, owner-consent-documented, compounded-from-bulk, telemedicine-only-contact, …) and generate the case base, borrowing only the *method* (factors as binary pro-party premises; hierarchy of intermediate factors; Horty's superset/subset constraint) from this literature.

## 4. Regulatory source material for a veterinary constitution

### 4.1 Copyright ground truth (verified)

- **US federal government works are public domain.** [usa.gov/government-copyright](https://www.usa.gov/government-copyright): a work created by a US government officer or employee as part of official duties is not subject to copyright. This covers the **CFR, the Federal Register, FDA/CVM guidance documents, DEA materials, USDA/APHIS regulations, and openFDA data**.
- **The same designation does NOT extend to state and local government.** Same page: *"The U.S. government work designation does not apply to works of state and local governments. Those works may be protected by copyright."*
- **State statutes and regulations are nevertheless generally free** under the *edicts of government* doctrine, reaffirmed in *Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org* (2020) — no one can be the "author" of law having the force of law. In California specifically, *County of Santa Clara v. Superior Court (CFAC)*, 170 Cal.App.4th 1301 (2009) held that CA agencies generally lack authority to assert copyright over public records absent express statutory authorization. **Practical rule: state statutory and regulatory *text* → treat as free; state-agency *annotations, headnotes, formatting, and website chrome* → do not copy; commercial codifiers (Westlaw/Lexis/Thomson) → never copy.**
- **EU legal acts are reusable.** [European Commission legal notice](https://commission.europa.eu/legal-notice_en): reuse is governed by **Commission Decision 2011/833/EU**, and unless otherwise noted EU-owned content is available under **CC BY 4.0**, with attribution and indication of modification. Caveat: only the OJ version is authentic; third-party content and trademarks are carved out.

## 6. Which regulations should be ABSOLUTE provisions — and why

An **absolute provision** in this architecture is one that (a) is a bright-line rule rather than a standard, (b) admits no balancing against competing factors, (c) is machine-checkable from structured fields the PIMS already holds, and (d) has severe, non-monetary consequences on breach. It must therefore sit **above** the precedent ordering: no accumulation of prior decisions can license a violation, and a case that would require violating it is **inadmissible**, not merely losing.

**Tier 1 — ABSOLUTE (non-derogable, no distinguishing permitted)**

1. **21 CFR 530.41 — prohibited extralabel drug uses.** A closed, enumerated list (chloramphenicol, fluoroquinolones in food animals, sulfonamides in lactating dairy cattle, and the rest of the list). Enumerated prohibitions are the paradigm case of a rule that cannot be balanced. Breach risks human food-supply contamination. *Machine-check: drug identity × species × production class.*
2. **21 CFR 530.11 — ELDU prohibitions**, in particular ELDU in or on animal feed and any use producing residues that may present a risk to public health. Same rationale; third-party (human) harm, not client harm.
3. **21 CFR 1306.22 / 1306.12 — controlled-substance refill limits.** Schedule II: refills prohibited, full stop. Schedule III/IV: ≤ 5 refills and none more than 6 months after issuance. Purely arithmetic, zero interpretive latitude, federal criminal exposure. This is the cleanest absolute provision in the entire corpus and should be the system's canonical worked example.
4. **21 CFR 1306.04(a) — legitimate medical purpose in the usual course of professional practice.** Slightly more standard-like than the others, but it is the provision under which prescriptions are void *ab initio*; a decision that issues a prescription without it is not a wrong decision, it is a void act. Model it as absolute with a required affirmative finding.
5. **21 CFR 530.10 read with 530.3(i) — no ELDU without a valid federal VCPR.** The VCPR is the jurisdictional predicate for the whole extralabel regime. If it fails, the system has no authority to reach the merits — this is precisely an **admission rule**, not a merits rule. Note the live federal/state divergence (federal "recently seen"; CA §4826.6 telehealth) — encode the federal floor as absolute and the state expansion as a permission that cannot lower the federal floor for ELDU.
6. **21 CFR 1311 Subpart C — EPCS controls**: two-factor authentication for the signing function, identity proofing at AL3+, FIPS-validated digital signature with post-signature alteration prevention, daily audit-trail review, and two-person authorization for granting/revoking signing permissions. These are the provisions that bind the *software itself* rather than the clinician, and a practice-management system that fails them is non-compliant regardless of any user's intent. No balancing: a single-factor signing path is not "less compliant," it is unlawful.
7. **21 CFR 1304.04 + 1304.11 — 2-year record retention and biennial inventory.** Numeric, auditable, and the destruction of a record is irreversible — the harm cannot be cured after the fact, which is the strongest structural argument for absoluteness.
8. **EU AI Act Art. 5 — prohibited AI practices**, insofar as the PIMS ships AI features: no manipulative/subliminal techniques materially distorting owner behaviour, no exploitation of vulnerability (age, disability, socio-economic situation), no emotion inference on staff. Article 5 is drafted as an absolute list with no risk-management escape hatch; mirroring it as absolute is faithful to the source.
9. **GDPR Art. 22 — human intervention and the right to contest.** This one is absolute *about the Digital Judiciary itself*: if the institution makes solely-automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effect on an EU data subject, the appeal path and human review are legally mandatory. It should be encoded as a meta-provision that no precedent can erode.

**Tier 2 — binding but defeasible (subject to distinguishing and the precedent ordering)**

- 21 CFR 530.13 (compounding permitted when no suitable approved alternative exists) — an explicit necessity test, i.e. a standard.
- 21 CFR 530.20–530.25 (extended withdrawal intervals) — requires professional judgment about "extended."
- 21 CFR 558.6 VFD content and duration requirements — mostly checkable, but "approximate number of animals" and indication-matching admit judgment.
- 9 CFR Part 2 attending-veterinarian / adequate-veterinary-care provisions — classic standards.
- State PDMP query/report mandates — jurisdictionally variable; the natural home for the **lexicographic ordering** (same-state precedent > sister-state precedent).
- State practice-act scope-of-practice and delegation-to-technician rules.

**Tier 3 — advisory only (persuasive, never binding)**

- FDA CVM Guidance for Industry (#256, #256B, and the withdrawn #269) — guidance documents state FDA's current thinking and are explicitly non-binding. They are ideal for testing that the system distinguishes *authority* from *persuasion*.
- AVMA and AAVSB model acts — private model law, copyrighted, not enacted anywhere as such.
- AAHA standards.

**Deliberately excluded from vendored provision text**

- **PCI DSS** — verified non-redistributable. Encode as self-authored requirement statements citing requirement numbers only.
- **HIPAA** — encode as an explicit *non-applicability* provision citing 45 CFR 160.103, so that "HIPAA requires…" arguments are ruled inadmissible rather than tacitly absorbed.

## 8. Open gaps

1. **No licensed factor case base exists anywhere.** Build one natively for veterinary compliance.
2. **No consistency benchmark exists.** The AF-CBR-COMPAS inconsistency criterion is the only formal, empirically-applied definition found; re-implement it and publish the veterinary version.
3. **SARA's license is unstated** despite being the closest analogue to a machine-checkable constitution. Worth one email to the JHU authors — a confirmed permissive license would make it the best single design reference available.
4. **State veterinary board disciplinary decisions are the only source of *real* veterinary adjudications with facts + charges + dispositions**, and they are scattered across 50 state sites in PDF with inconsistent copyright postures. A per-state license review would unlock the highest-value precedent seeds in the domain.
5. **AJVR Supplementary Table S1** (states mandating veterinary PDMP participation) returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch and is AVMA-copyrighted; the underlying state-by-state facts must be re-derived from state agency pages directly.
