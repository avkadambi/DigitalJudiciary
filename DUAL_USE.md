# Dual-use note

**Tiered, 1 September 2026.**

This corpus pairs synthetic cases with **real regulatory provisions**. The cases are invented; the
obligations they engage are in force. That combination is what makes the corpus useful and it is
also what makes this note necessary.

## What the corpus contains that warrants care

For each provision it carries a worked account of what violating it would look like in a plausible
enterprise setting, and — in the `P-pressure` category — the social arguments a requester makes to
have the violation approved. The pressures are not novel attack techniques; they are ordinary
workplace situations, catalogued from the compliance literature. Documenting them is net
beneficial, which is the same judgement PACT reached about the same material.

## Tier 1 — encouraged

Conformance testing an implementation. Reproducing the dissertation's figures. Building or
evaluating governance architectures. Teaching. Regulatory and audit research.

## Tier 2 — permitted with attribution and care

Adapting the corpus to other domains or jurisdictions. Using it as a seed for generated corpora.
Benchmarking commercial systems, provided results name the corpus and its synthetic nature.

## Tier 3 — not an intended use, and asked against

Using the violation descriptions as a template for evading a real compliance control. Using the
pressure catalogue to design social-engineering material aimed at human approvers or at deployed
assistants. Presenting any figure derived from this corpus as a measurement of real-world
compliance, of a named vendor, or of a named model in deployment.

## What the licence does and does not do

The MIT licence does not enforce these tiers and is not intended to. This note states the authors'
intent so that a user who departs from it does so knowingly, and so that a reviewer can see the
question was asked rather than avoided. A restrictive licence was considered and rejected: it would
not prevent tier-3 use and would obstruct tier-1 use, which is the larger population.

## The residual risk, stated

The pressure catalogue is the part with genuine dual-use character. It is a list, written down in
one place, of the arguments that most reliably move a compliance decision. It is published because
the defence against those arguments is structural — a mechanism that cannot read them — and
structural defences are testable only if the attack surface is written down. That is the argument;
it is not a proof, and a reader who disagrees with it has a reasonable position.
